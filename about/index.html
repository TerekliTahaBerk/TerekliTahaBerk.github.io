About

A few more things.

Outside of pipelines and papers, most of what I do still comes back to the same instinct: check whether something is actually precise, or just sounds precise.

Mathematics, mostly

I'm finishing a B.Sc. in Mathematics at Yıldız Technical University in Istanbul (2022–2026). I didn't come to data and automation from a computer-science track — I came to it from proofs, and it shows in how I work: I'd rather define a metric carefully and report it honestly than ship something that looks impressive for a week.

That's the thread connecting a segmentation research project, a Redshift warehouse and an RPA bot: each one is really a question about what a precise definition should be, and what happens when the real world doesn't cooperate with it.

Where curiosity goes

I started in automation — RPA at Latro and Papara, wiring SAP and internal tools together with Robusta and Automation Anywhere. That work taught me to be suspicious of anything that "usually works," because a bot doesn't get to shrug at an edge case.

From there the pull was toward data: pipelines, warehouses, and eventually applied ML through the TÜBİTAK research project. I'm currently a data intern at Getir, and I spend a fair amount of spare time reading around applied machine learning and probability on Kaggle.

Volunteering

Alongside coursework and internships, I coordinate volunteers for two organisations — mostly logistics, scheduling and keeping projects moving rather than anything technical, but it's taught me a different kind of precision: the kind where the "system" is a group of people, not a pipeline.
